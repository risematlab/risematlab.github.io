Matias Leon-Miquel defends his doctoral dissertation

On June 22, Matias Leon-Miquel successfully defended his doctoral dissertation. He had taken part in MSU’s spring doctoral hooding ceremony earlier in the year.

"To boldly go where no man has gone before" — that's exactly what Matias's research is doing.

His dissertation, "Strain-Hardening Cementitious Composites from Extraterrestrial Regolith Simulants: Ductile Mooncrete and Marscrete for Space Construction," opens a new frontier in construction materials for humanity's exploration beyond Earth. With NASA - National Aeronautics and Space Administration's Artemis II Mission, the first crewed lunar flyby in 50 years, successfully completed just this past April, Matias's work on ductile, damage-tolerant composites made from regolith simulants couldn't be more timely.

Beyond his research, Matias was recognized as the first-ever 2026 Outstanding CEE Graduate Student Instructor at Michigan State University Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering and delivered an Ignite Talk on his work at Michigan State University. His research was supported by Michigan Space Grant Consortium.

We would also like to thank the thesis committee - Michael Velbel, Nizar Lajnef, and Weiyi Lu - for their guidance throughout Matias's doctoral journey, and the RISE lab team for their support. Multiple publications are on the way. Can't wait to see what's next for Matias! 🚀
