AI-aided ductile well cement

🎉 Thrilled to share the latest publication from our RISE Materials Lab — also selected as the front cover article in ACS Omega (ACS Publications) from American Chemical Society! "AI-Aided Design of Carbonated Ductile Well Cement for Enhanced Recovery"

Most wellbore cement research focuses on compressive strength, but cement sheaths in EOR/EGR wells fail in tension, driven by cyclic thermal loading, pressure fluctuations, and casing deformation. Traditional well cements are brittle. They crack, degrade, and leak. We asked: what if we could make well cement bend instead of break?

Our paper introduces a new class of fiber-reinforced, carbon-cured ductile wellbore cement that can bend instead of break, addressing these critical challenges in wellbore integrity for oil, gas, and geothermal energy infrastructure.

Key findings:
🔬 Our ductile wellbore cement transformed the failure mode from brittle fracture to ductile strain hardening, achieving a peak tensile ductility over 6% (600 times of conventional wellbore cement).
💨 CO₂ curing accelerated matrix densification, while stabilizing long-term ductility and preventing late-age embrittlement.
🤖 An interpretable AI framework quantitatively linked microstructural evolution to mechanical performance, turning chemistry into design guidance.

Congratulations to my PhD students Yinjian Li (lead author), Diego Aparicio Vallejos, and Brandon Byers from Michigan State University, our collaborator Tianyu Wang, and Fan Shi, Dustin Crandall, and Guoxiang (Gavin) Liu from National Energy Technology Laboratory.
