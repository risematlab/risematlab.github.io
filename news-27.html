RESEARCH · 2026-07-06

Lunar and Martian regolith research

📄 New publication out in npj Space Exploration (Nature Portfolio)! "Assessing the Cementitious Reactivity of Lunar and Martian Regolith Simulants for Extraterrestrial Construction"

If humanity is going to build on the Moon or Mars, we need to use what's already there. Shipping construction materials from Earth costs over $1.2 million per kilogram, making in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) not just practical, but essential.

With NASA's Artemis II Mission successfully completed just this past April, understanding how to build with lunar and Martian surface materials has never been more urgent. The key question: can the surface soil (regolith) act as a cement-like binder, or just a filler?

Through a multimodal reactivity assessment, we have the following key findings:
🔬 Regolith simulants, through thermal activation and/or mechanical grinding, shows no measurable hydraulic or pozzolanic reactivity.
🏗️ When blended with cementitious materials, the simulants could produce composites with compressive strengths suitable for semi-structural applications
🌕 Regolith works more as a filler and that could be sufficient to build with.

These findings establish a practical baseline for later-stage ISRU construction strategies, where regolith-derived fillers are used alongside synthesized binders for durable extraterrestrial infrastructure.

Congratulations to lead author and PhD graduate Matias Leon-Miquel and co-author Brandon Byers from RISE Materials Lab. This work was supported by NASA - National Aeronautics and Space Administration Michigan Space Grant Consortium. Full paper attached — open access!
