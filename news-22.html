A new review guides the design of heat-storing concrete

The Journal of Materials in Civil Engineering published a review of phase change materials in concrete online on October 11, 2026. Dr. Xiaoqiang Ni led the review with Wenyu Liao, Hongyan Ma, Álvaro Paul and Qingxu Jin. The authors examined how different ways of incorporating phase change materials (PCMs) affect heat storage, mechanical performance and durability.

RISE has been testing these materials for several years. In 2023, Ni presented experiments on how PCMs affect the thermal and mechanical properties of concrete at MSU. In October 2025, the team installed four experimental slabs on campus to see how its heat-storing, self-healing concrete would perform outdoors over the winter.

The review compares direct mixing, microencapsulation and porous carriers. For pavements, the authors highlight how PCMs could reduce temperature differences through the depth of a slab, limiting curling and thermal cracking. They also discuss how modeling can help researchers decide how much PCM to use and where to place it before field testing.
